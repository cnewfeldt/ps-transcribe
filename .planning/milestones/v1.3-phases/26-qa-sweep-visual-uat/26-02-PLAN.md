<objective>
Retire DICT-06 (the post-dictation clipboard restore mechanism). 26-01 Task 2 smoke-test surfaced that the 3-second restore breaks the user's mental model: they hit the dictation hotkey expecting the transcript on the clipboard, but it disappears 3s later — often before they've switched apps to paste. Routed in-phase per D-02 (small, focused, no architectural change).

Decision: Option A from the QA discussion — delete the restore entirely. Dictation is now a normal copy operation; the privacy markers (org.nspasteboard.TransientType + AutoGeneratedType) remain so Maccy/Alfred still skip the entry.

After this lands, 26-01 sweep resumes from Task 2 with working dictation→clipboard→paste behavior, unblocking QA-01, QA-02, QA-03a (and the QA-05 update flow).
</objective>

<context>
- 26-01 Task 2 smoke test failed: dictation transcribed correctly but clipboard was empty by the time user could Cmd-V.
- Verified via `pbpaste; sleep 1; pbpaste; sleep 4; pbpaste` — text appeared mid-window then was restored.
- Root cause: `scheduleClipboardRestore(after: clipboardRestoreDelay=3.0s)` in `DictationCoordinator.endDictation` (line 243), implementing DICT-06 from `.planning/milestones/v1.2-REQUIREMENTS.md:20`.
- DICT-06 origin: Pitfall #6 / `18-CONTEXT.md:145` — concern that user might copy something between dictation-end and pasting; restore was meant to "preserve their real clipboard."
- UAT finding: the assumed concern is much less common than the harm of vanishing clipboard contents.
</context>
